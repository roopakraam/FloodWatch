## Related work {#sec:related}

**Thresholding.** The classical way to map water in a radar image is to threshold the backscatter, with the threshold chosen automatically, for instance by Otsu's method, which picks the cut that best separates a histogram into two groups [@otsu1979threshold]. Rule-based chains of this kind are still used operationally, and they remain a serious reference: Bereczky et al. benchmarked convolutional networks against such a chain for Sentinel-1 water and flood mapping [@bereczky2022sentinel].

**The dataset and its baseline.** Sen1Floods11 [@bonafilia2020sen1floods11] contains 4,831 Sentinel-1 chips of 512×512 pixels from eleven flood events. For 446 of them a water mask was drawn by hand; the others carry automatically generated ("weak") labels. Its authors trained a fully convolutional network with a ResNet-50 backbone on each label set, compared it with an Otsu threshold on the VH band, and reported the mean IoU over chips. Two of their findings matter here. First, the network trained on the hand labels was never their best model: on the test chips it stayed below the threshold (0.313 against 0.359), and only the network trained on ten times as many weak labels from Sentinel-2 was clearly better (0.408). Second, on the held-out Bolivia flood the threshold (0.386) was level with their best network (0.387). This project uses only the hand-labelled chips, so it works in exactly the small-data regime in which the authors' own network did not beat a threshold.

**Deep networks on Sen1Floods11.** U-Net [@ronneberger2015unet], an encoder–decoder with skip connections, is the standard segmentation architecture and most later work builds on it. Konapala et al. compared combinations of Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 inputs for U-Nets [@konapala2021exploring]; their radar-only U-Net is listed with a test IoU of 0.45 in the comparison table of Yadav et al. [@yadav2022attentive]. Katiyar et al. trained SegNet and U-Net models for near-real-time mapping [@katiyar2021near]. Bai et al. fused Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 in a boundary-aware network [@bai2021enhancement]. Closest to this project, Yadav et al. proposed an "Attentive U-Net" with a ResNet-50 encoder initialised from ImageNet that takes exactly the three inputs used as the default here (VV, VH and their ratio) and adds spatial and channel attention; it reaches a test IoU of 0.672, and a second network that also receives elevation and a permanent-water mask reaches 0.695 [@yadav2022attentive]. The same authors later used a pre-flood image as a second input [@yadav2022siamese].

**More data instead of bigger labels.** Patel et al. showed that self-supervised pre-training on unlabelled radar imagery improves a DeepLabv3+ on this dataset (test IoU 0.669 against 0.635 from random initial weights), and that semi-supervised training with unlabelled images helps when whole flood events are held out but not on the standard split [@patel2021evaluating]. Foundation models push this further: Prithvi, pre-trained on a large archive of optical imagery [@jakubik2023foundation], reaches a water IoU of 0.80 on the Sen1Floods11 test chips after fine-tuning [@prithvi2023sen1floods11], and Schmalstieg et al. distilled a 300-million-parameter successor into a 0.7-million-parameter student for edge devices [@schmalstieg2026distillation]. The fine-tuned Prithvi model reads the optical Sentinel-2 bands of the same chips, not the radar bands, so such results show what is possible when the sky is clear rather than what radar alone can do.

**Small models and classical learners.** MobileNetV2 [@sandler2018mobilenetv2] is a compact network built from depthwise-separable convolutions and inverted residual blocks, designed for phones; with ImageNet weights [@deng2009imagenet] it is a common light encoder. At the other end, Iselborn et al. trained classical learners such as gradient-boosted trees on per-pixel features; with radar features alone their models reach a pooled IoU of about 0.5 on the test chips [@iselborn2023importance], which is the level of a well-chosen threshold (@sec:results). Their paper is also careful about a point that makes published numbers hard to compare: some papers pool all pixels of the test set before computing IoU ("total IoU"), others average the IoU of each chip ("mean IoU"), and the two can differ by almost a factor of two on this dataset.

**How reliable are small differences?** Phunjanna et al. re-evaluated surface-water models on Sen1Floods11 and found that close rankings change with the random seed and with how regions are weighted [@phunjanna2026controlled]. That is the reason every configuration in this project is trained with three seeds, and why a difference is only called real when it exceeds the seed-to-seed spread.

**Where this project sits.** The studies above mostly add something: attention, extra inputs, more data, larger encoders. Few isolate the effect of the three most basic choices (input channels, loss, encoder) for a small model under a fixed protocol with repeated seeds, and few report what the choices cost on consumer hardware. That is the gap this project fills.
