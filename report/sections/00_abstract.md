**Abstract.** Radar satellites see floods through cloud, and deep networks have become the standard way to turn their images into water maps. This project asks what a small network can do under tight constraints, and which design choices matter. U-Nets with at most {{cost_default_params}} million parameters were trained on the {{n_train}} hand-labelled training chips of Sen1Floods11 on a laptop with a 4 GB GPU. One factor was varied at a time (input channels, loss, encoder), each configuration with three seeds, under a frozen protocol in which the test data were scored once per run. The default model, a MobileNetV2 U-Net fed with VV, VH and their ratio and trained with BCE + Dice, reaches a water IoU of {{default_test_iou_pm}} on the test chips, against {{otsu_test_iou}} for an Otsu threshold and {{tuned_test_iou}} for the best single threshold, and within a point of a published model with a much larger encoder. Adding VV to VH and using a Dice-based loss help; the ratio channel, the encoder size and ImageNet weights do not. On a flood never seen in training the lead disappears: no network beats the Otsu threshold reliably ({{default_bolivia_iou_pm}} against {{otsu_bolivia_iou}}), models with ImageNet weights are clearly worse ({{imagenet_bolivia_iou_pm}}), and the model that validation selects is the one that transfers worst. About a third of the best model's errors are water that is not visible in the radar image.
